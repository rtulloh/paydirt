Still open. (1) The offense-offended non-first-down branch still sorts by
nominal `(auto_first_down, yards)` and compares `yards > play_yards`. No engine
projection exists on that side: `_project_scrimmage_penalty_situation` returns

exists; AI-032 is its concrete open consequence and the sharper defect.

Evidence: 10 Hard-vs-Hard public-1983 games gave 43 defense-offended scrimmage
decisions, 15 offense-offended nominal decisions (13 enforced, 2 kept), 6
first-down comparisons, and no AI-032 shape; every offense-offended decision had
empty `penalty_situations`. Probes reproduced three wrong choices: 2nd-and-1 at
midfield, incomplete, DEF 5X enforced to 1st-and-10 five yards back instead of
keeping 3rd-and-1 at the same spot; a red-zone half-distance foul whose nominal
15 yards enforces 2-4 actual yards; and AI-032's 4th-and-10, 3-yard gain, OFF 5
enforced so the offense keeps the ball on 4th-and-15.

Assessment: minor-to-moderate decision-quality debt, not release-blocking. Each
choice is legal and no state or accounting is wrong; per-occurrence cost is
roughly 0.5-1.5 expected points in specific short-yardage and red-zone shapes.
Keep Medium and unscheduled. If scheduled: extend the engine projection to
DEF/PI options and a non-converting retained play, rank resulting situations
from the offense's side, and fix the AI-032 fallback in the same change. The
legacy expectations that encode the nominal policy,
`tests/test_computer_ai.py::test_accepts_penalty_when_auto_first_down` and
`::test_accepts_penalty_when_more_yards`, plus the comment at
`tests/test_cpu_penalty_decision.py:159`, would need real-fixture replacements
