## Description

Found in the 2026-11-14 review of the AI-031 late-game kneel-out guard. The guard at paydirt/computer_ai.py compared partial copies of the live state before either penalty choice was resolved. It advanced the spot and down but left the clock unchanged, so it missed enforcement choices that consume five seconds and then let the leading offense kneel out. Reproduced with public 2026 sample teams: Q4, offense leading 7-0, third-and-10 at midfield, defense out of timeouts, incomplete pass plus OFF 5. At 45 seconds, enforcing the foul leaves 40 seconds and can_kneel_out_game() is True; declining leaves fourth-and-10 and returns False. At 41 seconds the same failure occurs. The AI-side projection also duplicated timing rules and could drift from apply_penalty_decision: a retained foul charges flagged-play timing, not an ordinary 25-45 second scrimmage play, and deferred timeout/spike contexts must remain visible to the preview. The engine should own a side-effect-free projection of both choices, including resulting clock, period, game-over state, untimed downs and applicable deferred procedures, so the AI consumes one authoritative preview.

## Resolution / verification

- Add failing synthetic regressions for Q4/41s and Q4/45s boundary cases where enforcement consumes five seconds before the kneel-out decision.
- Project both penalty choices in the engine, including the resulting clock, period, game-over state, untimed downs and applicable deferred timeout/spike procedures, without changing live state, statistics, the play log or dice.
- Evaluate the late-game guard against those projected post-decision states rather than pre-play copies, and keep leading-defense, overtime, earlier-period and no-game-context controls unchanged.
- Add projection-versus-applied parity tests for retained and enforced choices and document the engine-owned preview in docs/ai-difficulty.md.

Fixed. Added an engine-owned projection for each scrimmage penalty choice
(`_project_scrimmage_penalty_situation`) and an engine rule for evaluating the
projected kneel-out (`can_kneel_out_penalty_situation`). The projection applies
enforcement or retained spot/down rules, charges the engine's flagged-play
timing, and records the resulting quarter, clock, game-over and untimed-down
state. A resolved scrimmage foul marks the clock stopped, so deferred timeout or
spike requests cannot save additional time. `PenaltySituation` keeps the
original spot/down equality because the new coaching fields are non-comparing.
The AI now projects both choices and calls the engine rule rather than copying
partial state.

Tests first: the parametrized 40/45/46-second regressions failed against the
prior pre-play guard at 45 seconds and now verify the post-decision clock. A
projection-versus-applied parity test failed with the retained projection at 20
seconds versus the applied 40 seconds; both retained and enforced choices now
match. A deferred defensive-timeout test verifies that a retained foul's
clock-stopping rule correctly prevents the timeout while the projection and live
resolution keep the same 34 seconds.

Validation with `.venv/bin/python3.12`: `tests/test_ai_penalty_defense.py` 37
passed. Full regression is still required for the final candidate; no commit or
